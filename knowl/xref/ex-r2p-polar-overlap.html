Exercise 18. A Region Common to a Cardioid and a Circle.

Solution.
Setting \(1+\sin\theta = \sqrt{3}\cos\theta\) gives \(\theta = \pi/6\text{,}\) where both curves have \(r = 3/2\text{.}\) To be inside both curves, a ray must stop at whichever curve it reaches first, so the boundary of \(R\) is the smaller of the two.
🔗
The circle only exists for \(-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2\text{,}\) so no part of \(R\) lies outside that range. On \(-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/6\) the cardioid is the smaller one; on \(\pi/6 \le \theta \le \pi/2\) the circle is. Hence
🔗
\begin{equation*} \iint_R f\,dA = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/6}\!\!\int_0^{1+\sin\theta} f\,r\,dr\,d\theta + \int_{\pi/6}^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^{\sqrt3\cos\theta} f\,r\,dr\,d\theta . \end{equation*}
Taking \(f=1\) gives the area of \(R\text{,}\) about \(1.057\text{.}\)
