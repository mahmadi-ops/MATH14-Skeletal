Exercise 16. An Integral Between Two Circles.

Solution.
Work in polar coordinates, where the integrand is \(r\) and the area element is \(r\,dr\,d\theta\text{.}\) Over the large disk,
🔗
\begin{equation*} \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{2a} r^2\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{2\pi(2a)^3}{3} . \end{equation*}
The small circle is \(r = 2a\cos\theta\) and is swept out for \(-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2\text{,}\) so the disk it bounds contributes
🔗
\begin{equation*} \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^{2a\cos\theta} r^2\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{(2a)^3}{3}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^3\theta\,d\theta = \frac{4(2a)^3}{9} . \end{equation*}
Subtracting, the integral over \(D\) is \(\dfrac{2\pi(2a)^3}{3} - \dfrac{4(2a)^3}{9}\text{.}\)
