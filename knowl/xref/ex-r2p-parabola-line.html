Exercise 17. A Double Integral Between a Parabola and a Line.

Solution.
Solve \(y+1 = \frac{y^2}{2}-3\text{,}\) that is \(y^2-2y-8 = 0\text{,}\) to get \(y=-2\) and \(y=4\text{:}\) the two curves meet at \((-1,-2)\) and \((5,4)\text{.}\) Slicing horizontally, the line is the right boundary and the parabola the left one, so
🔗
\begin{equation*} \iint_R k\,xy\,dA = k\int_{-2}^{4}\!\!\int_{y^2/2-3}^{\,y+1} xy\,dx\,dy = \frac{k}{2}\int_{-2}^{4} y\left[(y+1)^2 - \left(\tfrac{y^2}{2}-3\right)^2\right]dy = 36k . \end{equation*}
Integrating in the other order would need two separate integrals, because for \(x \lt -1\) the region is bounded above and below by the two halves of the parabola, while for \(x \gt -1\) the line takes over as the lower boundary.
