Exercise 6. A Solid Under a Slanted Plane*.

A solid \(D\) lies above the rectangle \(0 \le x \le 2\text{,}\) \(0 \le y \le 1\) in the plane \(z = 0\) and below the plane \(z = x + y + 1\text{.}\) Its density is constant, \(\delta = 1\) g/cm\({}^3\text{,}\) with \(x\text{,}\) \(y\text{,}\) \(z\) measured in cm.
🔗
The mass of the solid is \(M = \) g, and its center of mass \((\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z})\) has \(\bar{x} = \) cm, \(\bar{y} = \) cm, and \(\bar{z} = \) cm.
🔗

Solution.

The solid is \(0 \le x \le 2\text{,}\) \(0 \le y \le 1\text{,}\) \(0 \le z \le x + y + 1\) (Figure 4.13.8), and \(\delta = 1\text{.}\) By (2.8.10),
🔗
\begin{equation*} M = \int_0^2\!\!\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{x+y+1} dz\,dy\,dx = \int_0^2\!\!\int_0^1 (x + y + 1)\,dy\,dx = \int_0^2 \left(x + \frac32\right) dx = 2 + 3 = 5 \text{ g}. \end{equation*}
By (2.8.11), after the \(z\)-integration,
🔗
\begin{align*} M_{yz} \amp = \int_0^2\!\!\int_0^1 x(x + y + 1)\,dy\,dx = \int_0^2 \left(x^2 + \frac32\,x\right) dx = \frac83 + 3 = \frac{17}{3},\\ M_{xz} \amp = \int_0^2\!\!\int_0^1 y(x + y + 1)\,dy\,dx = \int_0^2 \left(\frac{x}{2} + \frac56\right) dx = 1 + \frac53 = \frac83,\\ M_{xy} \amp = \int_0^2\!\!\int_0^1 \frac{(x + y + 1)^2}{2}\,dy\,dx = \frac16\int_0^2 \left[(x+2)^3 - (x+1)^3\right] dx\\ \amp = \frac1{24}\Big[(x+2)^4 - (x+1)^4\Big]_0^2 = \frac{(256 - 81) - (16 - 1)}{24} = \frac{20}{3} . \end{align*}
By (2.8.12),
🔗
\begin{equation*} \bar{x} = \frac{17/3}{5} = \frac{17}{15} \approx 1.133, \qquad \bar{y} = \frac{8/3}{5} = \frac{8}{15} \approx 0.533, \qquad \bar{z} = \frac{20/3}{5} = \frac43 \approx 1.333, \end{equation*}
all in cm. The point \((\bar{x}, \bar{y})\) lies beyond the middle \((1, \frac12)\) of the base, toward the corner \((2,1)\) over which the solid is tallest, as it should.
