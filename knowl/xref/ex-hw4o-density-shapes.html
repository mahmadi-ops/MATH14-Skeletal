Exercise 1. Which Shapes Can Carry This Density?

With \(\delta(x,y) = x^2-y^2\) and the shapes of Figure 3.7.1: type the numbers of the two shapes that can carry \(\delta\) as a density, in increasing order and separated by a comma (for instance 1,3).

Solution.

A density cannot be negative, so we need \(x^2 - y^2 \ge 0\text{,}\) that is \(|x| \ge |y|\text{.}\) That holds on shapes \(2\) and \(4\text{,}\) and fails on shapes \(1\) and \(3\text{.}\)
🔗
Shape \(2\) is the triangle \(0 \le y \le x \le r\text{,}\) and shape \(4\) is the sector of the circle of radius \(r\) between the negative \(x\)-axis and \(y=-x\text{.}\) Since \(\delta(-x,y) = \delta(x,y)\text{,}\) shape \(4\) has the same mass as its mirror image in the \(y\)-axis, the sector between the positive \(x\)-axis and \(y=x\text{,}\) and that sector lies inside the triangle. As \(\delta \ge 0\) on the triangle, and \(\delta \gt 0\) on the part of it outside the sector, \(\iint_{R_2}\delta\,dA \gt \iint_{R_4}\delta\,dA\text{:}\) shape \(2\) is heavier. (In fact \(\iint_{R_2}\delta\,dA = \int_0^r\!\int_0^x (x^2-y^2)\,dy\,dx = \frac{r^4}{6}\text{,}\) while shape \(4\) has mass \(\frac{r^4}{8}\text{.}\))
