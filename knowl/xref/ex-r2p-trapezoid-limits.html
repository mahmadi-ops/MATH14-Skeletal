Exercise 15. Limits of Integration Over a Trapezoid.

Solution.
The trapezoid sits between the vertical lines \(x=1\) and \(x=3\text{.}\) Its top edge is the horizontal line \(y=4\text{.}\) Its slanted lower edge joins \((1,0)\) to \((3,2)\text{,}\) a line of slope \(1\) through \((1,0)\text{,}\) so that edge is \(y = x-1\text{.}\) Hence
🔗
\begin{equation*} \iint_R f\,dA = \int_1^{3}\!\!\int_{x-1}^{4} f(x,y)\,dy\,dx . \end{equation*}
With a constant integrand the integral is that constant times the area of \(R\text{,}\) and \(\int_1^3\left[4-(x-1)\right]dx = \int_1^3 (5-x)\,dx = 6\text{.}\)
