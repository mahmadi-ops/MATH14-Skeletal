    (b) Find the Cartesian coordinates of \(P\text{:}\) \(x = \) , \(y = \) .

    6. Mass Between Two Circles.

    Find the mass of the region bounded by \(x^2+y^2 = 2x\) and \(x^2+y^2 = 2y\text{,}\) whose density is \(\rho = 1/x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Away from the origin the two circles cross at the polar angle \(\theta = \) , and the mass is .
    🔗

    Solution.

    The two circles are \(r = 2\sin\theta\) and \(r = 2\cos\theta\text{,}\) meeting where \(\theta = \pi/4\text{.}\) With \(\rho = \frac{1}{r\cos\theta}\) and \(dA = r\,dr\,d\theta\) by (2.4.1),
    🔗
    \begin{align*} M \amp = \int_0^{\pi/4}\!\!\int_0^{2\sin\theta} \frac{r\,dr\,d\theta}{r\cos\theta} + \int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_0^{2\cos\theta} \frac{r\,dr\,d\theta}{r\cos\theta}\\ \amp = 2\int_0^{\pi/4}\tan\theta\,d\theta + 2\int_{\pi/4}^{\pi/2} d\theta = \ln 2 + \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 2.264 . \end{align*}
    🔗

    7. A Half-Disk Integral in Polar Form.

    Sketch the region of integration and evaluate by changing to polar coordinates:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \int_{9}^{18}\!\!\int_{0}^{\sqrt{18x - x^2}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\,dy\,dx . \end{equation*}
    Answer:
    🔗

    Solution.

    The curve \(y = \sqrt{18x - x^2}\) is the upper half of \(x^2 + y^2 = 18x\text{,}\) the circle of radius \(9\) centered at \((9,0)\text{,}\) which in polar form is \(r = 18\cos\theta\text{.}\) The condition \(x \ge 9\) is \(r\cos\theta \ge 9\text{,}\) that is \(r \ge 9\sec\theta\text{.}\) The vertical line and the circle meet where \(18\cos\theta = 9\sec\theta\text{,}\) so \(\cos^2\theta = 1/2\) and \(\theta = \pi/4\text{.}\) The region is the part of the upper half-disk to the right of \(x = 9\text{,}\) described by \(0 \le \theta \le \pi/4\text{,}\) \(9\sec\theta \le r \le 18\cos\theta\text{.}\) With \(dA = r\,dr\,d\theta\) by (2.4.1) and \(1/\sqrt{x^2+y^2} = 1/r\text{,}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \int_0^{\pi/4}\!\!\int_{9\sec\theta}^{18\cos\theta} \frac{1}{r}\,r\,dr\,d\theta \amp = \int_0^{\pi/4} \left(18\cos\theta - 9\sec\theta\right) d\theta\\ \amp = \Big[\,18\sin\theta - 9\ln\lvert\sec\theta + \tan\theta\rvert\,\Big]_0^{\pi/4} = 9\sqrt2 - 9\ln\!\left(1 + \sqrt2\right) \approx 4.796 . \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    8. Between a Circle and a Cardioid.

    Use a double integral in polar coordinates to find the area of the region bounded on the inside by the circle of radius \(5\) centered at the origin and on the outside by the cardioid \(r = 5(1 + \cos\theta)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Area:
    🔗

    Solution.

    The circle is \(r = 5\text{,}\) and it meets the cardioid where \(5(1+\cos\theta) = 5\text{,}\) that is \(\cos\theta = 0\text{,}\) \(\theta = \pm\pi/2\text{.}\) The cardioid is outside the circle exactly when \(\cos\theta \gt 0\text{,}\) so the region is \(-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2\text{,}\) \(5 \le r \le 5(1+\cos\theta)\text{,}\) and
    🔗
    \begin{align*} A \amp = \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\!\int_{5}^{5(1+\cos\theta)} r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{25}{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \left[(1+\cos\theta)^2 - 1\right] d\theta\\ \amp = \frac{25}{2}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2} \left(2\cos\theta + \cos^2\theta\right) d\theta = \frac{25}{2}\left(4 + \frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 50 + \frac{25\pi}{4} \approx 69.635 . \end{align*}
    🔗
