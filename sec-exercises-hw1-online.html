    1. Describing a Motion, I.

    An object moves along a curve so that at time \(t\) it has coordinates
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = \frac{3t}{\sqrt{t^2+1}}, \qquad y(t) = \frac{3}{\sqrt{t^2+1}}. \end{equation*}
    Describe the motion.
    🔗
    Identify the curve the object travels on: it is a (type line, parabola, circle, ellipse, or hyperbola).
    🔗
    Then graph the path and indicate the direction of motion. There is no box for this part: include the graph in the solution you submit to the grader.
    🔗

    Solution.

    For every \(t\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} x^2 + y^2 = \frac{9t^2}{t^2+1} + \frac{9}{t^2+1} = \frac{9(t^2+1)}{t^2+1} = 9, \end{equation*}
    so the object stays on the circle of radius \(3\) centered at the origin. Since \(y(t) = 3/\sqrt{t^2+1} > 0\) for every \(t\text{,}\) only the upper half of that circle is ever visited.
    🔗
    As \(t \to -\infty\text{,}\) \(x \to -3\) and \(y \to 0\text{,}\) so the object comes in from near \((-3,0)\text{;}\) at \(t = 0\) it is at \((0,3)\text{,}\) the top of the circle; and as \(t \to \infty\) it approaches \((3,0)\text{.}\) Because \(x'(t) = 3/(t^2+1)^{3/2} > 0\text{,}\) the \(x\)-coordinate always increases, so the object moves steadily from left to right over the top of the circle: clockwise, as in Figure 2.1.1. It never reaches the two endpoints \((\pm 3, 0)\text{;}\) it only approaches them.
    🔗
    The speed is \(\lVert\mathbf{v}(t)\rVert = 3/(t^2+1)\text{:}\) the object is fastest at the top, at \(t=0\text{,}\) and slows down as it nears either endpoint.

    2. Describing a Motion, II.

    An object moves along a curve so that at time \(t\) it has coordinates
    \begin{equation*} x(t) = \sin t - 2, \qquad y(t) = 4 - 2\cos t, \qquad 0 \le t \le 2\pi. \end{equation*}
    Describe the motion.
    🔗
    Identify the curve the object travels on: it is a (type line, parabola, circle, ellipse, or hyperbola).
    🔗
    Then graph the path and indicate the direction of motion. There is no box for this part: include the graph in the solution you submit to the grader.
    🔗

    Solution.

    From \(x = \sin t - 2\) and \(y = 4 - 2\cos t\) we get \(\sin t = x + 2\) and \(\cos t = (4 - y)/2\text{,}\) so
    \begin{equation*} (x+2)^2 + \frac{(y-4)^2}{4} = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1. \end{equation*}
    The path is the ellipse centered at \((-2,4)\) that extends \(1\) unit left and right of the center and \(2\) units above and below it.
    🔗
    Following the object through one period: at \(t=0\) it is at \((-2,2)\text{,}\) the bottom of the ellipse; at \(t=\pi/2\) at \((-1,4)\text{,}\) the rightmost point; at \(t=\pi\) at \((-2,6)\text{,}\) the top; at \(t=3\pi/2\) at \((-3,4)\text{,}\) the leftmost point; and at \(t=2\pi\) it is back at \((-2,2)\text{.}\) Bottom, right, top, left is counterclockwise, so as \(t\) runs from \(0\) to \(2\pi\) the object goes once around the ellipse counterclockwise, starting and ending at its lowest point (Figure 2.1.2).

    7. Where Is the Object After Travelling 15?

    The path of an object is
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{r}(t) = \left\langle 12t,\, 8t^{3/2},\, 3t^2 \right\rangle , \qquad t \ge 0, \end{equation*}
    starting at the origin. Find where it is after travelling a distance of \(15\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Its speed at \(t=0\) is .
    🔗
    The object has travelled a distance of \(15\) at time \(t =\) .
    🔗
    At that moment it is at the point \(\langle\) , , \(\rangle\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Solution.

    Differentiating, \(\mathbf{v}(t)=\left\langle 12,\ 12t^{1/2},\ 6t\right\rangle\text{,}\) and the speed collapses to a perfect square:
    \begin{equation*} \left\lVert\mathbf{v}(t)\right\rVert = \sqrt{144+144t+36t^2} = \sqrt{36(t+2)^2} = 6(t+2). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Hence the distance travelled by time \(t\) is
    \begin{equation*} s(t)=\int_0^{t} 6(\tau+2)\,d\tau = 3t^2+12t , \end{equation*}
    🔗
    and \(3t^2+12t=15\) gives \(t^2+4t-5=(t+5)(t-1)=0\text{,}\) so \(t=1\text{.}\) The object is then at \(\mathbf{r}(1)=\left\langle 12,\ 8,\ 3\right\rangle\text{.}\)
