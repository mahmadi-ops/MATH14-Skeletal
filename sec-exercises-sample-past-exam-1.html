    1.

    The helix starting from \((2, -2, 0)\) is parametrized as
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{r}(t) = \left\langle 2\cos(t),\, -2\cos(t),\, 2\sqrt{2}\sin(t) \right\rangle . \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the arc length \(s(t)\) of the helix, measured from \((2, -2, 0)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The velocity is
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{v}(t) = \left\langle -2\sin t,\, 2\sin t,\, 2\sqrt{2}\cos t \right\rangle, \end{equation*}
    so the speed is
    \begin{equation*} \lVert \mathbf{v}(t) \rVert = \sqrt{4\sin^2 t + 4\sin^2 t + 8\cos^2 t} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}. \end{equation*}
    The starting point \((2,-2,0)\) is \(\mathbf{r}(0)\text{,}\) so
    \begin{equation*} s(t) = \int_0^t \lVert \mathbf{v}(\tau) \rVert \, d\tau = \int_0^t 2\sqrt{2}\, d\tau = 2\sqrt{2}\, t . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Reparametrize the helix with respect to arc length.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Solving \(s = 2\sqrt{2}\, t\) for \(t\) gives \(t = \dfrac{s}{2\sqrt{2}}\text{,}\) hence
    \begin{gather*} \mathbf{r}(t(s)) = \Bigl\langle\, 2\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{s}{2\sqrt{2}}\Bigr),\; -2\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{s}{2\sqrt{2}}\Bigr),\\ \qquad\qquad 2\sqrt{2}\,\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{s}{2\sqrt{2}}\Bigr) \Bigr\rangle . \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Find the total mass of a wire in the shape of the parabola \(y = x^2\) for \(\sqrt{2} \le x \le \sqrt{6}\text{,}\) where \(x\) and \(y\) are measured in centimeters. Assume that the mass density of the wire is given by \(\rho(x,y) = y/x\) in grams per centimeter.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Parametrize the wire by \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, t^2 \rangle\text{,}\) \(\sqrt{2} \le t \le \sqrt{6}\text{.}\) Along it the density is
    \begin{equation*} \rho\bigl(x(t), y(t)\bigr) = \frac{t^2}{t} = t, \end{equation*}
    and \(\mathbf{v}(t) = \langle 1, 2t \rangle\text{,}\) so \(\lVert \mathbf{v}(t) \rVert = \sqrt{1 + 4t^2}\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{align*} M \amp = \int_C \rho(x,y)\, ds = \int_{\sqrt{2}}^{\sqrt{6}} t\sqrt{1 + 4t^2}\, dt\\ \amp = \frac{1}{8} \int_{\sqrt{2}}^{\sqrt{6}} 8t\sqrt{1 + 4t^2}\, dt\\ \amp = \frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{2}{3} \left(1 + 4t^2\right)^{3/2} \Bigg|_{\sqrt{2}}^{\sqrt{6}}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{12}\left(5^3 - 3^3\right) = \frac{98}{12}, \end{align*}
    so the mass is \(M = \dfrac{98}{12} = \dfrac{49}{6}\) grams.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Evaluate the line integral \(\displaystyle\int_C 5y\, dx - \sin(y)\, dy\text{,}\) where \(C\) is the counter-clockwise oriented unit circle.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Take \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle \cos t, \sin t \rangle\text{,}\) \(0 \le t \le 2\pi\text{,}\) so that \(\mathbf{v}(t) = \langle -\sin t, \cos t \rangle\text{.}\) The field is \(\mathbf{F} = \langle 5y, -\sin(y) \rangle\text{,}\) which along \(C\) is \(\mathbf{F} = \langle 5\sin t, -\sin(\sin t) \rangle\text{,}\) and
    \begin{align*} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{v} \amp = -5\sin^2 t - \cos t \, \sin(\sin t)\\ \amp = -\frac{5}{2}\bigl(1 - \cos(2t)\bigr) - \cos t \, \sin(\sin t). \end{align*}
    Hence
    \begin{align*} \int_C 5y\, dx \amp - \sin(y)\, dy\\ \amp = \int_{0}^{2\pi} \Bigl[ -\tfrac{5}{2}\bigl(1 - \cos(2t)\bigr) - \cos t \, \sin(\sin t) \Bigr] dt\\ \amp = \Bigl[ -\tfrac{5}{2}\bigl(t - \tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2t)\bigr) + \cos(\sin t) \Bigr]_0^{2\pi}\\ \amp = -\frac{5}{2}\,(2\pi) = -5\pi . \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Find a parametrization of the curve of intersection of the following surfaces.
    🔗

    (a)

    \(z = 3x + y\) and \(y = 5x^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let \(x = t\text{.}\) Then \(y = 5t^2\) and \(z = 3t + 5t^2\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} x(t) \amp = t,\\ y(t) \amp = 5t^2,\\ z(t) \amp = 3t + 5t^2, \qquad t \in \mathbb{R}. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    \(x^2 + y^2 = 9\) and \(z = 2x + y\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The cylinder gives \(x = 3\cos t\text{,}\) \(y = 3\sin t\text{,}\) and then \(z = 2x + y = 6\cos t + 3\sin t\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} x(t) \amp = 3\cos t,\\ y(t) \amp = 3\sin t,\\ z(t) \amp = 6\cos t + 3\sin t, \qquad 0 \le t \le 2\pi. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    True or False? Clearly justify your answer. Note that correct answers with incorrect or unclear justifications will not earn credit.
    🔗

    (a)

    Consider the line integral \(\displaystyle\int_C f\, ds\text{,}\) where \(f\) is a scalar function. Reversing the direction in which the integral is taken along a curve changes the sign of the line integral, \(\displaystyle\int_{-C} f\, ds = -\int_C f\, ds\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    False. Let \(C\) be \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle x(t), y(t) \rangle\text{,}\) \(a \le t \le b\text{.}\) Then \(-C\) is traced by \(\mathbf{r}(a+b-t) = \langle x(a+b-t), y(a+b-t) \rangle\text{,}\) \(a \le t \le b\text{,}\) and with the substitution \(u = a+b-t\text{,}\) \(du = -dt\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} \int_{-C} f\, ds \amp = \int_{a}^{b} f\bigl(\mathbf{r}(a+b-t)\bigr)\, \lVert \mathbf{v}(a+b-t) \rVert\, dt\\ \amp = \int_{u=b}^{u=a} f\bigl(\mathbf{r}(u)\bigr)\, \lVert \mathbf{v}(u) \rVert\, (-du)\\ \amp = -\int_{u=b}^{u=a} f\bigl(\mathbf{r}(u)\bigr)\, \lVert \mathbf{v}(u) \rVert\, du\\ \amp = \int_{u=a}^{u=b} f\bigl(\mathbf{r}(u)\bigr)\, \lVert \mathbf{v}(u) \rVert\, du = \int_C f\, ds . \end{align*}
    The line integral of a scalar function does not change sign when the direction is reversed.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    If \(f(x,y,z) = \cos\left(x^2 + y^2 + z^2\right) - 2\) and \(C\) is a smooth curve from point \(A\) to point \(B\text{,}\) then \(\displaystyle\int_C f(x,y,z)\, ds \gt 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    False. Since the cosine is at most \(1\text{,}\) \(f(x,y,z) \le -1\) everywhere, and therefore
    \begin{equation*} \int_C f(x,y,z)\, ds \lt 0 . \end{equation*}
    🔗

    6.

    Let \(C\) be a circle centered at the origin. Also, assume that the vector field \(\mathbf{F}\) is the vector field which is graphed in Figure 3.5.1.
    🔗

    (a)

    If \(C\) is traced counterclockwise, is \(\displaystyle\int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}\) positive, negative, or zero? Explain.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Negative. Along the upper half of \(C\) the counterclockwise unit tangent \(\hat{\mathbf{T}}\) points leftward while \(\mathbf{F}\) points right; along the lower half \(\hat{\mathbf{T}}\) points rightward while \(\mathbf{F}\) points left. So the angle \(\theta\) between \(\hat{\mathbf{T}}\) and \(\mathbf{F}\) is obtuse everywhere along \(C\text{,}\) which means \(\mathbf{F} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{T}} \lt 0\text{,}\) and hence
    \begin{equation*} \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{T}}\, ds \lt 0 . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Is \(\mathbf{F}\) conservative? Explain.
    🔗
    Solution.
    No. If \(\mathbf{F}\) were conservative, we would have \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0\) for any loop \(C\text{,}\) and part (a) found a loop around which the circulation is not zero.
